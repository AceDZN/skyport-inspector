Visible controls, rider/cube transport, recovery and all correct/wrong/contrast routes still need built UI and normal-input proof before these drafts become accepted playable levels. The shared scene remains art-owned. No old fifty-room fixture is restored.

All rooms stay enclosed, with solid ceilings/walls, dark exit vestibules and truthful white portal surfaces. Alternative instructions reuse exactly the same fixture, inventory, start state and wiring. Each door/circuit performs a visible physical function. ## 6 · Keep the Circuit

**Mission:** Do not take the cube off the button. Use the north door.

**Situation.** A maintenance fork with a raised north gallery wraps around a glazed power alcove. The only cube starts on `north-button`, physically holding `north-door` open. The cube can be picked up normally. A second `south-button` is on the other side of the service lobby and opens `south-door`; both doorways have genuine vestibules and distinct north/south destinations. Opaque switchback corridors make the two branches visually different from the parallel glass bays in room 3. Eligible portal bays link the working alcove/lobby, not the space beyond either closed exit door.

**Normal solution.** Observe the already-powered north trace and open shutter; leave the cube pressing it, use a portal pair to leave the glazed working area, then traverse the north maintenance corridor. This tests restraint after several retrieval puzzles. It must still require active spatial navigation, not a menu choice followed by a straight walk.

**Wrong attempt/recovery.** Picking up the cube clears pressure and closes the requested north door; use that visible consequence for concise feedback. Put it back or use the local normal reset. Do not punish an earlier corrected experiment with an invisible permanent “touched cube” failure. No claim is made that final state alone proves an entirely violation-free reading attempt. Space the button away from exit safety zones so player pressure plus a sprint cannot replace the intended maintained circuit.

**Same-fixture counterfactual:** Move the cube to the south button. Use the south door. The cube genuinely changes buttons; the powered shutter and complete route change. Both placements remain possible before and after the wording change. Proposed IDs: `cube`, `north-button`, `south-button`, `north-door`, `south-door`, `north-exit`, `south-exit`.

**Support/acceptance.** Existing cube/pressure/shutter/portal mechanisms; no new mechanic asserted. Prove default north and changed south with normal inputs, plus actual removal→close→replacement recovery. Test both doorways/return access and block portal placement into dark exit vestibules geometrically.

## 7 · A Bridge at Rest

**Mission:** You mustn't send the platform east. Cross to the north dock.

**Situation.** A T-shaped transfer hall surrounds a safe catch well. A solid stopped transfer deck starts at a north stop, joining the central hub to `north-dock`; its east stop joins a different east dock. The deck translates between two actual positions, rather than changing a route label. The deck-mounted panel is reachable from the fixed hub at either alignment. Catch-basin stairs return a stranded player to that hub; no unimplemented remote-call control is assumed. A glazed initial control booth needs a portal pair to reach the hub, while the destination vestibules remain dark and cannot be skipped by a direct portal.

**Normal solution.** Recognize that the parked deck is already a bridge, leave the east command alone and walk the north connection. The novelty is a real device one does not always need to activate. Keep readable deck/dock alignment and a stable standing surface, without a tight timing race.

**Wrong attempt/recovery.** Sending it east removes the north connection visibly. Its deck-mounted panel remains reachable from the fixed hub at either stop, so the player can select north again; a fall reaches actual catch-basin stairs back to the hub. No separate fixed call widget is assumed. Do not use a secret historical “ever sent east” flag. A local retry can begin a clean language attempt; restoring the platform must restore physical access.

**Same-fixture counterfactual:** You can send the platform east. Cross to the east dock. Moving the real deck now creates the useful east connection. Can grants permission; the destination sentence gives the operational reason to move. This is not a claim that can itself means must. IDs: `transfer-deck`, stops `north`/`east`, `north-exit`, `east-exit`; one control mounted on the real deck.

**Support/acceptance.** Requires native horizontal carrier, passenger/cube contacts, selectable stops, visible E controls and safe recovery. Author as a stopped `lift` with horizontal stop positions if that is the native selectable carrier API; a continuously cycling moving-platform is unsuitable for a “keep it parked” instruction. Prove solid deck/gap widths, both actual alignments, normal riding/walking, recall and the no-move default route. No new carrier appearance before the generated target is inspected.

## 8 · Upper Freight

**Mission:** You must take the cube to the upper dock. Leave it on the button.

**Situation.** A tall freight shaft has a lower loading room and an upper receiving gallery, not another flat cube alcove. The platform has two selectable vertical stops and one reachable deck-mounted control. Lower and upper receiving buttons actuate separate real exit shutters. The lower cube can be carried on the lift or delivered through a valid portal configuration; clever independent delivery is allowed. Portal surfaces must be truthful and not give direct access beyond a closed exit.

**Normal solution.** Transport the cube to `upper-button` on the upper dock, leave it there, then use `upper-exit`. The physical pressure holds that gallery's door open. Reaching the gallery without cargo leaves the requested door closed. Upper docking clearance must fit a capsule plus the original cube; do not raise difficulty by narrowing apertures.

**Wrong attempt/recovery.** Leaving the cube on the lower button opens the genuine lower exit, not the requested upper door. The player can retrieve the cube and correct delivery by the actual deck/control or eligible gallery portal route. There are no fictional fixed recall controls: a missed ride must be tested for access to the deck, a valid portal recovery or the ordinary local retry.

**Same-fixture counterfactual:** You must leave the cube at the lower dock. Put it on the button. Use `lower-button`/`lower-exit` instead, with the same cube/lift/upper option still available. The contrast changes actual destination and circuit, not a cargo ID. Proposed IDs: `freight-lift` with stops `lower`/`upper`, `cube`, `lower-button`, `upper-button`, `lower-door`, `upper-door`, `lower-exit`, `upper-exit`.

**Support/acceptance.** Needs native vertical carrier, actual rider/cube collision, ceiling/landing clearance and recovery from both stops. Test lower/upper delivery and both named real doors. Do not grade whether the cube was held, rode alongside the player or crossed independently; only actual delivered pressure and the requested destination matter.

## 9 · A Choice of Cargo

**Mission:** You can use either cube. Open the east exit.

**Situation.** A broad inspection atrium offers two identical cubes with distinct retrieval routes: one visible in a low sealed glass cell, the other on a raised service gallery reached via a carrier/portal arrangement. Their masses/sizes/accepted pressure are equivalent. The east exit uses one real button. The west exit has two distinct buttons wired with a real AND. Both exits, labels, traces and circuits exist in the fixed blueprint; optional cargo must never be spawned from the current sentence.

**Normal solution.** Choose either cube, use its real retrieval route and press the east button to open the east door. At least two normal solutions must be tested. Can is permission from the displayed facility instruction, not a claim that only one cube can physically move. Either does not secretly forbid retrieving the second cube as well.

**Wrong attempt/recovery.** A player who assumes both are mandatory does extra work but has not violated a permission. Accept the east solution; feedback may explain that either was sufficient, without denying progression. Sending a single cube to one west input leaves the visible AND's west door closed; the cube can be moved back to the east input. There is no wrong-language penalty for a valid permitted option.

**Same-fixture counterfactual:** You must use both cubes. Open the west exit. Now retrieve both and occupy the two west inputs simultaneously. Either assignment is valid, and removing one genuinely closes the west shutter. Do not count earlier button touches or select an unstated “correct” cube. Proposed IDs: `cube-a`, `cube-b`, `east-button`, `west-button-a`, `west-button-b`, `east-door`, `west-door`, `east-exit`, `west-exit`.

**Support/acceptance.** Uses the tested pressure/AND pattern but adds a permission-based choice across two different retrieval routes. Carrier support must be verified if used in the upper route. The room must visually differ from room 5, with a broad central inspection aisle, remote storage faces and opposed exit branches. Prove each cube individually opens east; one cube cannot activate both separated west buttons; two cubes open west; both assignments and recovery work.

## 10 · The Optional Ride

**Mission:** You don't have to ride the lift. Put the cube on the upper button.

**Situation.** A multilevel service shaft has a broad stepped perimeter approach and a two-stop lift. Both are real usable ways to reach the upper receiving area with the cube. A second side gallery is separated by a six-metre gap that the parked upper lift deck physically bridges; an alignment circuit actuates its actual side door only while the lift is properly docked. The deck-mounted control is reachable from the adjacent lower landing before sending it up, so parking does not require riding. The stepped route is deliberately generous enough for the cube; no fake railing or impossible clearance may invalidate the permission.

**Normal solution.** Either ride with the cube or take the stepped route and place it on `upper-button`, opening `upper-door`. Do not penalize riding: don't have to removes an obligation and does not mean mustn't. A valid portal delivery is acceptable if it satisfies the real receiving state. No hidden route choice is graded.

**Wrong attempt/recovery.** Misreading optional as forbidden may lead to unnecessary stair travel, but the stairs remain a valid solution. Misreading it as mandatory may produce an unnecessary ride, also valid. A genuine operational miss is delivering to the wrong height or moving the deck away from the side connection; use its real mounted control, the stairs or valid portal access to recover. Never invent a failed attempt merely because the player chose an allowed route.

**Same-fixture counterfactual:** You must park the lift at the upper dock. Use the side exit. Now the solid parked deck and true dock-alignment input are needed for the side connection. The player may operate the mounted panel while standing on the lower landing and use the stairs, so even this wording must not require a hidden ride event. The upper-button route remains physically present. IDs: `service-lift`, stops `lower`/`upper`, real derived dock flag `at:service-lift:upper`, `cube`, `upper-button`, `upper-door`, `side-door`, `upper-exit`, `side-exit`.

**Support/acceptance.** Needs selectable lift stops, truthful stopped-at-dock state, carrier contacts and reachable mounted control. The alignment flag derives from actual platform position/stop, never from the mission or a remembered button press. Native inspection confirms the side door opens only after actual arrival and closes on departure. Normal-input proof must still validate stair/lift base solutions, cube fit, parking without riding plus the side walk, moving deck removing the connection and safe recovery.

The five missions are original game examples. Source grades stay internal and do not create grade gates. This batch targets short practical reading and action planning, not free grammar production, whole chapters 10–12, all grades' proficiency, listening/speaking or retention. Permission legitimately widens the set of successful actions; inventing a single answer would misteach it. Where physical discovery alone can solve a necessary state, success does not independently prove language comprehension. Preserve this limit in coverage reports.
