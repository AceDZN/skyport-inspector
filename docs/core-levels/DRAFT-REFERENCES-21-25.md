## 21 · Two Jobs — pressure and optical obstruction

**Base:** “Move the cube that blocks the beam. Keep the button that powers the lift pressed. Reach the upper gallery.”

**Contrast:** “Keep the cube that blocks the beam in place. Move the cube that presses POWER onto SERVICE. Use the lower passage.”

**Physical problem:** A compact enclosed split-level plant has two initially identical cargo bodies with genuinely different live jobs: one interrupts an always-on low beam; the other presses POWER. A glazed partition allows inspection of both jobs and the actual receiver/lift. The upper route requires **receiver lit AND POWER pressed**. A real SERVICE pad opens the lower passage only while **receiver dark AND POWER unpressed**. Both branches have their own thick doorway and safe walking floor. Clear cargo without standing in its optical path, preserve pressure, board the lift and reach the upper floor; contrast reallocates pressure while leaving the real obstruction.

**Wrong choice/recovery:** Removing the pressure cube first brakes the lift; removing the optical cube under contrast keeps the lower passage closed. Both cubes remain reachable, with a non-reset ground return. Replace pressure or restore an obstruction and correct the allocation. A swap of equivalent cubes that produces the requested actual conditions is accepted; no particular cargo ID, “first moved” record or untouched-body history is checked.

**Language:** `that blocks` and `that powers` have the described noun as their subject. The clause identifies a causal job, not a shape decal. Optional Hebrew: “איזו קובייה חוסמת בפועל, ואיזה כפתור מפעיל את המעלית?”

## 22 · Follow the Supply — identify a machine by its circuit

**Base:** “Cross on the platform that the window receiver powers.”

**Contrast:** “Cross on the platform that the floor button powers.”

**Physical problem:** An enclosed over-and-under fork has two actual platforms: a receiver-powered lift to a high glazed bay and a pressure-powered horizontal carrier to a low recessed bay. Their real supply paths are visible from a common observation landing; neither “platform” is identified by a mission-colour lamp. The source/return portal pair directs the beam to the window receiver. A loose cube can maintain the independent floor button. Each branch has a real stopped-dock shutter and its own destination. The high and low banks cannot be exchanged by walking across a decorative rail.

**Wrong choice/recovery:** Powering or boarding the other machine leads to the other bay without unlocking the requested objective. Both docks permit return on the next cycle, with a bounded catch and real stair/lift return for misses. Switching the optical or pressure supply has an immediate physical effect. Equivalent physical arrival at the requested live bay remains valid; no remembered ride or machine-use flag is required.

**Language:** In `that the window receiver powers`, **receiver** is the clause subject and **platform** its object. The whole platform phrase follows `on` in the main instruction; that does not make platform the clause subject. Both variants end in *powers*: a compass word or repeated destination label cannot select the answer. This is a circuit-link puzzle, not two lifts merely labelled “receiver” and “button.”

## 23 · Screen and Door — distinguish cause from affected object

**Base:** “Keep the shutter that the receiver controls open. Take the passage behind it.”

**Contrast:** “Keep the shutter that blocks the beam closed. Take the passage beside it.”

**Physical problem:** A baffled single-floor optical workshop has an **input screen** that physically occludes the beam and a separate **output shutter** powered by its receiver. A real reachable pressure control opens the input screen. A linked portal pair supplies the optical turn around an opaque corner. Opening the screen lights the receiver, opens the output passage and closes a dark service bypass. Leaving the screen closed keeps the output unpowered and the side bypass open. All three leaves are ordinary physical shutters; the player must trace the real causal chain instead of trying E on an automatic output leaf.

**Wrong choice/recovery:** Acting on the wrong part leaves the requested passage closed or exposes the other route. The pressure control and portal surfaces remain reachable from the common vestibule. Releasing/replacing pressure restores the opposite branch; door safety must prevent crushing without letting an approaching body reopen a closed leaf. Destination access depends on current light/pressure, not an earlier correct interaction.

**Language:** Receiver **controls shutter** in the object relative; shutter **blocks beam** in the subject relative. *It* refers to the shutter identified by the full preceding clause. The clauses name actual different parts of one causal system, not two observational signs. The base and contrast must have clearly different “behind” and “beside” passages in the inspected target.

## 24 · Two Openings — limited omitted-object recognition

**Base:** “Use the passage the timer opens. Leave the manual switch off.”

**Contrast:** “Use the passage the manual switch opens. Leave the timer unpowered.”

**Physical problem:** An enclosed clock chamber contains a periodic airlock and a separately controlled static service passage around a central opaque pier. A cube initially powers the real timer through a pressure pad; a manual switch controls the other leaf. The timed route needs **timer power AND its actual open phase AND manual off**. The manual route needs **manual on AND timer power absent**. These are visible maintained circuits, not a time score. A familiar portal link brings the player from a glazed control booth into the safe waiting vestibule; neither far corridor is portalable.

**Wrong choice/recovery:** Choosing the manual route while the timer still has power leaves it closed; a missed periodic window can wait for the next cycle. Both wrong passages allow a return to the shared vestibule/control area. Stop/reallocate actual timer pressure and switch state to correct the branch; there is no irreversible error memory.

**Language:** `passage + the timer + opens` is an object relative with omitted *that*. The timer is the subject; the passage is what it opens. Optional paused help may expand it to “the passage **that** the timer opens.” Adding/removing *that* preserves meaning and must **not** become the supposed meaning-changing variant. The contrast changes the actual opening agent and maintained supply condition.

## 25 · Freight Return — combine identification with spatial planning

**Base:** “Put the cube the carrier transports on LIFT. Keep the button that opens the freight hatch pressed. Reach the balcony.”

**Contrast:** “Put the cube that holds the freight hatch open on SERVICE. Leave the other cube aboard. Use the lower return.”

**Physical problem:** A sealed freight loop has one cube initially travelling on a real loose-cargo carrier and another maintaining the hatch pressure. The carrier crosses a dry enclosed well to a retrieval dock behind that hatch. The player must plan an actual trip to fetch cargo and a return with it, using the existing walking portal/cargo mechanics; a portal view through glazing must show genuine reachable white surfaces. LIFT and hatch pressure together power the balcony lift. SERVICE with hatch pressure absent opens a separate lower return loop. The hatch closes physically when its support is removed; its near control remains reachable so a mistaken pickup is repairable. This is an acquisition-and-return problem, not a second beam-blocker layout.

**Wrong choice/recovery:** Picking up the hatch cube early closes the freight route; replacing it restores access. Missing a carrier docks or lands in a bounded catch with a real return. Under contrast the hatch cube can be reallocated without needing to fetch the travelling cube. Both cargo bodies remain ordinary interchangeable rigid bodies. If a clever swap yields the same genuine maintained pressure and requested destination, accept it; do not add provenance or exact cargo-assignment locks.

**Language:** `the cube the carrier transports` has an omitted object-relative word and a real independent subject, **carrier**. `the cube that holds...` has the cube as relative-clause subject. This combines the earlier patterns, with optional Hebrew explanation of who carries what. The carrier must actually carry its cargo in normal play; a static cargo label or replay claim is insufficient. Literal preservation of the original cube's identity is not independently graded.
