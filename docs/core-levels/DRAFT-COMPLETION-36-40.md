## 36 · One Release, One Journey

**Base:** “Release the cargo hatch, then complete the lift transfer to the gallery.”

**Contrast:** “Release the cargo hatch, then deliver the cube to STORE. Leave the gallery lift unpowered.”

**Physical assembly:** A tall enclosed service shaft separates a fast latched cargo hatch from a slower actual freight lift. A momentary TEST receiver releases the hatch. The retrieved cargo can maintain either LIFT pressure for ascent or STORE pressure for a lower stockroom route. STORE with LIFT absent opens the lower door; LIFT supplies the upper transport. Both supplies/routes always exist. A safe upper waiting floor prevents a missed lift phase becoming a trap.

**Ordinary chain:** route a real pulse → watch full hatch clearance/catch → retrieve cargo → place it on the chosen supply → wait for actual carriage/delivery → enter the corresponding physical destination. “The hatch has opened; the lift is still rising” is truthful only after the first opening while the second is actually moving. The first finished subtask cannot stand in for stopped upper support.

**Wrong consequence/recovery:** Cargo on STORE leaves the gallery lift braked; cargo on LIFT leaves the lower stockroom closed. Replace the cargo, wait for a real dock and continue. The open hatch remains open because of its latch, not the perfect report. Verify real opening versus in-progress ascent, safe waiting, source loss and both corrected routes.

## 37 · Arrived Is Not Stored

**Base:** “Finish both deliveries. Keep STORAGE and RELEASE pressed, then use the archive lift.”

**Contrast:** “Keep RELEASE pressed and leave STORAGE empty. Use the service passage.”

**Physical assembly:** A two-height enclosed cargo rack has two ordinary cubes and two receiving plates on separated balconies. RELEASE opens access to the second cargo bay; STORAGE plus RELEASE continuously powers the archive lift. RELEASE with STORAGE absent powers a lower service passage. Use a real latch for access to the second bay so a removed first cube remains recoverable; final transport still depends on actual pressure.

**Ordinary chain:** transport the first cube through a portal to RELEASE → retrieve the second by the now-real access/lift route → allocate the two bodies to both pads or maintain RELEASE alone. “Cargo has reached STORAGE” can be reported at genuine contact, but after removing it NOW says EMPTY; the old delivery is not a substitute for present support. Either cube can press either pad.

**Wrong consequence/recovery:** Reusing a delivered cube prematurely removes its supply and the archive lift stops; restore a second independent support or choose the requested service allocation. Cargo stays accessible on receiving floors. Verify both deliveries with swapped identities, a genuine removal/replacement and the physical AND versus service branch. This assesses distinguishing completed arrival from a maintained storage task, not counting deliveries.

## 38 · Stopped Is Not Finished

**Base:** “Complete the crossing to FAR DOCK. Keep the receiver lit while the carrier moves.”

**Contrast:** “Park the carrier at SERVICE. Use its stopped deck to cross the service walkway.”

**Physical assembly:** An enclosed L-shaped transport well has a selectable carrier with real LOADING, SERVICE and FAR stops. SERVICE aligns with a side walkway; FAR meets a separate bay. Actual receiver power enables motion and its loss brakes in place. Dock leaves use actual stopped alignment, never a selected destination. A low catch return permits an interrupted journey to be corrected without Retry.

**Ordinary chain:** configure the optical link → select the requested stop with visible E → board or reach its side landing → wait for real travel/stop → cross on supported geometry. A midrail power loss must say “The carrier has stopped. NOW: BETWEEN DOCKS,” not “has arrived.” At a real far arrival it may report that the crossing has reached FAR; the player still walks off safely to the actual bay.

**Wrong consequence/recovery:** A SERVICE stop does not complete the FAR route; a midrail brake does not open either dock leaf. Restore light and use the original physical control to finish, or wait at a safe landing for its return. No teleport to clock phase or hidden `journeyComplete` flag is allowed. Verify all three native poses, vector carriage, braking/restart, jump detachment and actual disembark seams before a target is accepted.

## 39 · Placed Is Not Connected

**Base:** “Complete the receiver connection. Clear the beam and leave the cargo on RELEASE before taking the lift.”

**Contrast:** “Leave the receiver dark. Put the cargo on SERVICE and use the inspection passage.”

**Physical assembly:** A staggered glass-sided optical workshop puts an ordinary cube on a small moving freight deck across the beam's path. Two placed portals alone do not guarantee a connection: a cube or solid housing can still intercept the ray. Collecting the travelling cube and parking it on RELEASE both clears the optical route and opens access to the receiver-powered lift. SERVICE with receiver darkness opens a different pedestrian loop. If the player discovers a geometrically valid alternative beam route, it remains legitimate.

**Ordinary chain:** place actual portals → observe whether the real receiver lights → fetch/reposition the obstructing cargo → maintain the requested pad and current light state → use the corresponding route. The journal may record “Both portals have been placed” while NOW says RECEIVER DARK; it must not call this a completed connection. After a true lit transition it can report the connection, with current lit/dark state updating independently.

**Wrong consequence/recovery:** An apparently complete but occluded pair leaves the lift off; an incorrect pad leaves the desired access shut. Retrieve, drop and realign without deleting cargo or clearing history. Verify genuine optical occlusion, stable freight pickup, both pad branches and corrected actual connection. An event projection supports reading; current physical light and pressure remain the cause.

## 40 · Retrieval Is Only the First Operation

**Base:** “Retrieve the cube, power the gallery lift and complete the gallery delivery.”

**Contrast:** “Retrieve the cube, leave the gallery lift unpowered and finish the SERVICE delivery.”

**Physical assembly:** A high enclosed retrieval vault is reached through a genuine measured momentum route. It contains one ordinary cube and a floor-aligned walking portal surface for the cargo return. Back at the observation level, cargo on GALLERY powers a separate final lift; cargo on SERVICE with GALLERY absent opens a lower transfer door. The first fling/retrieval is not the final destination. Do not make the cargo inaccessible after a miss; a basin/lift or broad stair return must reach the launch approach.

**Ordinary chain:** use a physically sufficient drop/launch → land safely at the vault → pick up actual cargo → carry it through a fitting walking pair → release it on the requested pad → take the resulting final transport. “You have reached the vault” reports a real location/event; NOW still shows the final supply empty. Completion follows actual final delivery/access, not a retrieval animation or launch flag.

**Wrong consequence/recovery:** A safe vault landing without cargo return leaves the final lift off. Wrong final allocation powers the other real route and is recoverable by regrasping/replacing the same cube. All valid momentum equivalents and cargo identities are accepted. This assembly depends on closed momentum, held-cargo aperture and native landing/catch regressions; it must remain unimplemented if those bounds are not established.
