## Make language causal

Use the existing Workbook chapter 10–12 source pack for grades 7–9 and retain exact internal provenance. Player-facing progression uses level numbers, never grade gates.

Possible sequence, adjusted to the available source content and tested mechanics:

| Levels | English demand | Physical consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–5 | Short imperatives and spatial language | The instruction identifies an action, its object and destination; players apply it in space. |
| 6–10 | Negation, obligation and permission | Competing valid routes differ in whether they obey the actual operational constraint. |
| 11–15 | Before, after, while and until | Door/platform/beam circuits require the stated order or maintained condition. |
| 16–20 | Comparatives and modifiers | Different drop heights, speeds, distances or cube properties matter to the chosen route. |
| 21–25 | Relative clauses and reference | Several plausible objects/controls exist; the description identifies the intended one. |
| 26–30 | Present versus past event information | Current states and physical machine history jointly identify the working route. |
| 31–35 | Passive voice and agent/patient roles | Beam/cube/door relationships must be interpreted to connect a real circuit. |
| 36–40 | Completed versus unfinished actions | Persistent machine states and event records determine which operation remains necessary. |
| 41–45 | Zero/first conditionals and time clauses | Players predict, configure and use actual conditional machinery. |
| 46–50 | Reported instructions and mediation | Combine real spatial mechanics and transfer concise instructions between linked subsystems. |

These are design directions, not a demand to force every grammar topic into an unsuitable interaction. A well-connected pair of topics is preferable to a contrived task.
